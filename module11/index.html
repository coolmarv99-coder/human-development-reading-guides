Maya's story

Different routes can support the same needs

Maya talks with an older classmate who returned to college after years of employment and family caregiving. Their educational timelines differ, yet both are working toward competence, connection, and purpose.

The conversation helps Maya notice how social clocks can make one timetable seem normal or required. In reality, adult pathways are shaped by identity, culture, relationships, resources, opportunity, and chosen commitments.

Maya begins comparing pathways without ranking them. The better developmental questions concern what is changing, what each person wants to preserve, and what conditions support autonomy, competence, and relatedness.

Reading lens: Compare pathways without treating timing as a scorecard. Look for shared developmental needs within different lives.

Adult pathways combine roles, relationships, opportunities, constraints, and choices rather than following one required timetable.
