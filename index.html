Maya’s Story

During a child development center observation, Maya watches a toddler stand in front of a low mirror. At first, the child smiles and waves at the “baby” in the reflection. Then the caregiver gently points to a sticker on the toddler’s shirt and says, “That’s you!” The toddler looks down, touches the sticker, and laughs.

Later, the same toddler insists on carrying a small cup to the table without help. The caregiver stays close, offers encouragement, and lets the child try.

Maya realizes that autonomy does not mean toddlers no longer need adults. Independence grows best when adults provide safe limits, patience, and opportunities to practice.

Reading lens: What does the caregiver do that supports both safety and independence?
