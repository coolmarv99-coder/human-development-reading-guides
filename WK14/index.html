Maya's story

Observe before you conclude

At a community garden, Maya interviews Mr. Ramirez about what he has learned through decades of growing food. In a noisy tool shed, he asks Maya to repeat one question and briefly loses track of where he placed a packet of seeds.

A volunteer whispers that these moments prove he should stop leading garden activities. Maya is not convinced. The setting is loud, the task is interrupted, and one observation cannot establish a pattern.

Maya chooses to improve the immediate conditions, listen carefully, and distinguish ordinary variability or sensory barriers from changes that deserve further attention.

Reading lens: Separate a respectful immediate response from diagnosis, and look for patterns, context, function, and appropriate supports.

Sensory access can change communication and performance; careful assessment is more informative than assumptions based on age.
