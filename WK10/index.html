Maya's story

A decision with several good reasons

Maya meets with a college counselor because she is considering transferring to a university. She is excited by the program, but she also works many hours and helps her family with transportation and household responsibilities.

The counselor does not tell Maya which choice to make. Instead, they compare priorities, identify constraints, and look for information that could turn a vague dilemma into several workable paths.

Maya begins to see mature decision-making as more than choosing the single abstractly correct answer. Good judgment can integrate evidence, values, relationships, timing, and changing circumstances.

Reading lens: Notice how adults coordinate practical constraints, personal values, multiple perspectives, and uncertain information.
